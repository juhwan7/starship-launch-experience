# Visual reference baseline — 2026-09

The visual target is **real Starbase / Starship photography and official SpaceX launch footage**, not a generic sci-fi rocket aesthetic.

## Primary visual references

### SpaceX — Starship Flight 13
https://www.spacex.com/launches/starship-flight-13

Use as reference for:
- Pad 2 launch lighting and late-afternoon exposure.
- Starship/Super Heavy scale relative to the tower.
- Engine-start brightness and expanding exhaust.
- Liftoff framing and ascent over the Gulf.
- Max-Q / ascent pacing.
- Hot-staging and post-separation motion.

Flight 13 lifted off at 5:51 p.m. CT on July 24, 2026, so the baseline look should read as a bright natural late-afternoon outdoor scene rather than a dark cinematic night scene.

## Visual direction derived from references

1. **Exposure**
   - Outdoor structures must remain readable in shadow.
   - Stainless steel should reflect sky and ground without becoming chrome-white.
   - Flame illumination should brighten nearby ground/smoke rather than leaving the environment black.

2. **Ground**
   - Avoid a single flat brown/gray plane.
   - Separate soil, service road, concrete pad, wet/darkened regions and scorched areas.
   - Add fine roughness variation and service/tire marks.

3. **Industrial materials**
   - Distinguish painted structural steel, raw/galvanized pipe, stainless tanks and concrete.
   - Add roughness/albedo micro-variation instead of flat colors.

4. **Camera**
   - Prioritize wide establishing and long-lens-like shots.
   - Keep Starship and the tower readable together before close-ups.
   - During ascent, use wider tracking so the vehicle scale and surrounding atmosphere remain legible.
   - During separation, show both stages and sufficient surrounding Earth/space.

5. **Motion**
   - No static-looking holds.
   - Even when vehicle translation is small, maintain cloud drift, vapor, flame turbulence, subtle camera drift, reflected-light changes and Earth rotation.

These references guide appearance only. The page remains a real-time reconstruction, not a reproduction of SpaceX copyrighted video footage.
